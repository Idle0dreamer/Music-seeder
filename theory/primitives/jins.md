# Jins primitive

## Definition

A jins is a tonicization with a characteristic set of interval relations,
tonal emphases, register, neighboring baggage, cadences, entry/exit behavior,
and melodic vocabulary.

It is not:

- an unordered pitch-class set;
- necessarily a tetrachord;
- an interval array with a name;
- proved by touching its nominal notes;
- independent of its maqām context.

## Typed structure

\[
J=(\tau,G,R,F,C,B,L,E,X,I,\Sigma)
\]

- \(\tau\): primary tonic;
- \(G\): ghammaz/secondary-tonic candidates and their functions;
- \(R\): characteristic and allowable register;
- \(F\): focal/emphasis roles;
- \(C\): core interval-relation constraints;
- \(B\): baggage tones and their functions;
- \(L\): lexical melodic cells and motif families;
- \(E\): entry formulas and prerequisites;
- \(X\): exit/cadence formulas and transition affordances;
- \(I\): contextual intonation model;
- \(\Sigma\): scope and evidence.

## Core versus baggage

Core and baggage are not separated only by range.

Core tones:

- are sufficient for clear local identity when used with appropriate behavior;
- participate strongly in tonicization and characteristic cells.

Baggage tones:

- occur outside the conventional compact span;
- remain governed by the same tonicization;
- can prepare or imply another jins without establishing it;
- may differ according to the containing maqām.

Each baggage entry states its direction, function, allowed duration, and
compatible motifs. A generic “extended scale” is invalid.

## Jins occurrence

A jins occurrence is a time interval with evidence:

\[
O_J=[t_0,t_1],\quad
q_J=P(J,\tau,R\mid x_{t_0:t_1},c)
\]

It is possible to have:

- an implied jins without its tonic sounding;
- ambiguous overlap between two interpretations;
- brief color without tonicization;
- full local tonicization;
- an expanded jins that still does not become a new maqām.

## Identity evidence

Evidence features include:

- recurrence and duration near the proposed tonic;
- characteristic approach and cadence formulas;
- emphasis distribution;
- register and contour;
- compatible baggage;
- expected intonation relations;
- legal source/target pathway;
- phrase position and closure;
- motif-family likelihood.

Interval match alone receives low weight.

## Catalog requirement

Every jins catalog entry must include audio-localized examples of:

- clear establishment;
- characteristic core motion;
- below-tonic baggage;
- above-ghammaz extension;
- at least one cadence;
- at least one ambiguous or negative example;
- each claimed transition.
