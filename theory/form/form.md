# Form and rhythmic context

Form is a separate layer that conditions the tonal and phrase grammars.

## Free taqsim form

A flexible taqsim plan may contain these functions:

- introduction and establishment;
- body or development;
- intensification or extended development;
- return and conclusion.

These names are descriptive, not four mandatory equal sections. A selected
profile supplies:

- which functions are required;
- admissible ordering and recurrence;
- typical but non-binding duration ranges;
- tonal and motivic obligations;
- boundary and cadence expectations.

The form planner produces a partially ordered section plan. The phrase grammar
realizes it and may revise the plan when accumulated musical evidence supports
an alternative licensed path.

## Free time

Non-metric performance is hierarchically timed, not uniformly random:

\[
T=\text{section span}\circ\text{phrase breath}\circ
\text{gesture elasticity}\circ\text{attack timing}.
\]

The timing model represents:

- phrase-internal acceleration and relaxation;
- hesitation and suspension;
- grouping by gesture;
- phrase-final lengthening;
- breath-like silence;
- local repeated-note regularity;
- rubato correlated with melodic function.

## Iqa

Iqa is an independent rhythmic system:

\[
I=(C,B,A,V)
\]

- \(C\): cycle and subdivision structure;
- \(B\): Dum, Tak, silence, and other beat-role sequence;
- \(A\): accent and timing profile;
- \(V\): licensed variations.

A maqam does not intrinsically own an iqa. A composed or song-like realization
selects an iqa through repertoire and form context. The melodic grammar then
aligns phrase accents, cadences, and ornaments with beat roles without treating
Dum and Tak as mere MIDI velocities.
