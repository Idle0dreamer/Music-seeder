# Sayr

Sayr is a profile- and maqam-specific policy over tonal space and formal time.
It is not a universal pitch contour.

## Representation

A sayr model is

\[
\mathcal{S}=(V,E,O,L,U,\Psi)
\]

- \(V\): landmark states, including root-jins establishment, register zones,
  emphasized tones, secondary ajnas, climax zones, and return states;
- \(E\): licensed moves between landmarks;
- \(O\): partially ordered obligations;
- \(L\): local residence and evidence requirements;
- \(U\): optional, repeatable, or skippable excursions;
- \(\Psi\): phrase-function and section-dependent weights.

An obligation is not necessarily one event. For example, “establish the root
jins” may require an idiomatic cell, emphasis and return around its tonic, and
a local cadence.

## Landmark state

\[
v=(j,r,d,e,b,h)
\]

- \(j\): active jins hypothesis;
- \(r\): register zone;
- \(d\): directional tendency;
- \(e\): emphasized role;
- \(b\): boundary or cadence expectation;
- \(h\): evidence threshold before departure.

## Paths

Valid performances are paths through a weighted finite-state controller with
memory:

\[
P(v_{t+1}\mid v_t,H_t,C)
\]

where \(H_t\) contains completed obligations, revisits, phrase functions,
motif history, and elapsed formal time.

This permits:

- delayed expansion after prolonged root-jins establishment;
- upward or downward development according to profile;
- multiple local peaks rather than one forced climax;
- return and renewed departure;
- abbreviated or extended forms;
- sectional re-exposition;
- alternative licensed conclusions.

## Climax

A climax is a discourse event, not simply the highest frequency. Its score uses:

\[
\operatorname{climax}(t)=
w_r R_t+w_i I_t+w_d D_t+w_n N_t+w_h H_t+w_b B_t
\]

with register, intensity, duration, novelty, perceptual salience, and
formal preparation. Costs are declared by profile and context and may be
calibrated from reviewed counts. A high note without preparation may be merely
an excursion.

## Return

Return requires a legal tonal path and restoration evidence:

- a root-jins-compatible cell or motif;
- weakening of the temporary center;
- recovery of characteristic root-jins emphasis;
- an appropriate cadence;
- sufficient phrase time and perceptual separation.

The final resolution cannot be generated by reversing an ascent mechanically.

## First executable subset

The first executable controller does not assign universal stage names or
probabilities. It represents partial order and alternative completion routes
exactly:

\[
\mathcal P=(i_\mathcal P,O,R)
\]

where each obligation is:

\[
o=(i_o,N_o,A_o).
\]

- \(i_o\): stable obligation identity;
- \(N_o\): a nonempty conjunction of typed landmark needs;
- \(A_o\subseteq O\): obligations that must already be complete.

The need vocabulary reads only retained typed state:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Jins}(j),\quad&
\operatorname{Center}(c),\\
\operatorname{Role}(r,n),\quad&
\operatorname{Region}(z,n),\\
\operatorname{Gesture}(g,n),\quad&
\operatorname{Phrase}(f,b,k?,n),\\
\operatorname{Path}(p),\quad&
\operatorname{Evidence}(e,q).
\end{aligned}
\]

Counts \(n\) are positive. Evidence threshold \(q\) is positive rational.
Phrase need \(k?\) optionally requires a cadence family. Role and region needs
count retained structural events; gesture and phrase needs count completed
identity-bearing spans. No need inspects a scale array, absolute frequency, or
waveform state.

A completion route is:

\[
r=(i_r,T_r),\qquad \varnothing\ne T_r\subseteq O.
\]

The route is complete when every terminal in \(T_r\) is complete. Predecessor
closure is already guaranteed by obligation fulfillment. Multiple routes
represent licensed abbreviated, extended, or alternative conclusions without
making one universal sequence mandatory.

## Fulfillment and ordering

Sayr state is:

\[
Q=(C,H)
\]

with completed identity set \(C\) and ordered completion history \(H\). The
checked transition is:

\[
\frac{
i_o\notin C\quad
A_o\subseteq C\quad
\Sigma\models N_o
}{
\operatorname{Fulfill}(i_o):
(C,H)\mapsto
(C\cup\{i_o\},H\cdot[(i_o,\Pi_o)])
}
\]

where \(\Pi_o\) retains typed proofs naming the exact jins, center, events,
phrase spans, gesture occurrences, paths, and evidence quantities that
satisfied \(N_o\).

This is how ordering is assessed:

1. the obligation graph must be acyclic;
2. every predecessor must appear earlier in completion history;
3. the current typed state must satisfy the complete need conjunction;
4. a candidate is complete only when at least one declared route is complete.

Container order, numeric enum order, pitch height, and seed do not establish
sayr order. The seed may choose between complete legal routes; it may not skip a
predecessor or manufacture a landmark.

## Climax boundary

The executable subset can prove that a profile-declared climax obligation was
prepared and fulfilled through its typed needs. It does not yet implement the
continuous climax score above. A neutral fixture may require an established
root, completed upper-register gesture, baggage event, phrase boundary, and
ordered predecessors. That proves controller mechanics, not a regional climax
definition.

## Regional reconstruction and generation

The plan is declarative authority; the generator is its consequence checker.
Regional reconstruction may change obligations, dependencies, needs, and
routes only through provenance-bearing profile data that still passes plan
validation. The generator must then show which complete candidates remain
legal and retain exact failures for missing landmarks or order violations.

The immediate neutral consumer has two routes:

- establish and settle locally;
- establish, expand, mark a prepared climax, travel, return, and cadence.

Its constants are fixtures, not claims about Egyptian, Levantine, Iraqi,
Turkish, North African, Gulf, or other practices.

## Executable laws

- incomplete, repeated, dangling, or cyclic obligation graphs fail admission;
- route identities and terminals are complete and unique;
- plan and obligation storage order do not change meaning;
- fulfillment before a predecessor fails without state mutation;
- a missing typed need retains the failing need identity;
- completion proofs retain exact source identities and rational evidence;
- repeated fulfillment fails;
- scope restoration prevents local sayr progress from leaking;
- candidate completion requires at least one declared route;
- profile narrowing can remove a route only through the musical action or
  landmark it forbids, with the diagnostic retained.

## Downstream breadth

This subset unlocks non-greedy phrase-route generation, explicit return debt,
alternative formal lengths, path-conditioned climax and closure, and later
well-founded recursive phrase productions. The next sequence must consume it
in the staged generator before recursive production budgets are added.
